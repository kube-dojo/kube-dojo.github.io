## Ashby's Homeostat (1948-1956)

| Item | Value | Verification |
|---|---|---|
| Designer | W. Ross Ashby (Director of Research, Barnwood House Hospital) | **Green** — Ashby 1960 *Design for a Brain* title page. |
| Documented article publication | Ashby, "Design for a Brain," *Electronic Engineering* 20:379-383 (December 1948) | [Reviewed metadata](ashby-publication-evidence-2026-09-05.md) establishes the publication record, not its contents or first-publication priority. |
| Book-length treatment | Ashby, *Design for a Brain* (Chapman & Hall / Wiley, 1952; 2nd ed. 1960 with rewritten latter two-thirds) | **Green** — full PDF of 2nd ed. on archive.org. |
| Companion volume | Ashby, *An Introduction to Cybernetics* (Chapman & Hall, 1956) | **Green** — full PDF on PCP / VUB. |
| Macy presentation | Ninth Macy Conference (1952), New York: "Homeostasis" talk | **Green** — Pias 2003 p. 593+. |
| Construction (Part A) | Four interconnected units, each carrying a pivoted magnet on top whose angular deviation from center forms one of four main variables. Each unit also has ancillary coils, potentiometers, water trough with electrodes at each end providing a potential gradient. | **Green** — Ashby 1960 *Design for a Brain* §8/2 pp. 100-101; Ashby 1956 §5/14 pp. 83-84. |
| Construction (Part B) | A relay (energized or not) and four 25-position stepping-switches. Each switch position carries a resistor of some value. Total Part B states: 2 × 25⁴ = 781,250. | **Green** — Ashby 1956 §5/14 p. 83. |
| Coupling rule | The relay is non-energized when and only when A is stable at the central positions. With the relay energized, none of B's states is equilibrial (switches keep moving); with the relay not energized, all are equilibrial (switches stay where they are). | **Green** — Ashby 1956 §5/14 p. 83. |
| Operation | When perturbed, B's stepping switches scan random configurations until A finds central equilibrium under that B configuration; B then stops and "remembers" by holding the switch positions. | **Green** — Ashby 1956 §5/14 p. 83-84. |
| Ashby's claim | The Homeostat demonstrates that an electromechanical system can find equilibrium configurations by searching part-function-rich state spaces — a model for adaptive behavior in organism-environment couplings. | **Green** — Ashby 1956 §5/14 + Ashby Macy 1952 talk in Pias 2003 p. 593-598. |
| Ashby's careful disclaimer | The adjective "homeostatic" derives from Cannon's prior coinage "homeostasis," not from Ashby's machine. | **Green** — Ashby 1960 *Design for a Brain* §8/2 footnote, p. 100. |

## Operational Limits Worth Naming in Prose

The following apparatus descriptions remain unrefreshed research claims. They must not be used to infer why the whole field failed to converge on a programmable thinking-machine project. The accepted cards support only the bounded institutional framing specified below.

- **The AA predictor was task-specific.** The light-spot-tracking apparatus was wired for the specific problem of curve extrapolation under aerial-evasion conditions. Reprogramming it to play chess or solve calculus would have required not new code (there was no code) but a different set of analog circuits. The physical structure *was* the program.
- **The Homeostat was task-specific.** Part B's stepping switches with 781,250 possible configurations searched only in the space of internal-coupling parameters — they could not be reprogrammed to control a different external variable space. Ashby explicitly notes that the machine demonstrates "ultrastability," not flexible computation.
- **The Macy meetings produced no shared programming-language analogue.** The conferences predate LISP (McCarthy 1958-1960) and FORTRAN (1957). When attendees discussed feedback systems they discussed circuit diagrams, block-flow descriptions, and transcript-quoted reasoning — not code. There was no medium for collaborative programming, and there was no expectation that there should be.
- **Restricted participation and edited publication were distinct practices.** In the [inspected passage](macy-format-evidence.md), Pias describes disciplinary parity, rejected participation requests, and editing discussions for publication. This distinguishes access to the room from access to its published record; it does not establish readership, movement-wide diffusion, or a recruitment counterfactual. The packet does not refresh the earlier claims about the surviving record of conferences 1-5.
- **Wiener's personal refusal is a separate evidence question.** The existing account of his October 1945 letters to de Santillana and Compton and his 1947 *Atlantic* article remains unrefreshed. The [aftermath card](aftermath-evidence.md) does not establish that his refusal removed funding for the whole field, nor does it establish a comprehensive funding inventory. Kline's inspected account instead links the later U.S. institutional difficulties and fragmentation to damaged scientific reputation and patronage changes, including reduced support in the 1970s; this chronology cannot explain the Macy series ending in 1953.

## Notes

- The [aftermath evidence](aftermath-evidence.md) supports attributed interpretation, not a newly inspected primary-record causal synthesis. Kline's later U.S. account includes continuing, overlapping specialties. Pickering's conceptual comparison of symbolic cognition and feedback-oriented models also includes digital computing within Wiener's program; it does not establish a hardware-determined outcome. These limits do not prove that apparatus or meeting restrictions had no historical effect.
